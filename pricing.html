02

Offensive mode is granted, not bought

Default deny

Off on every account

REX's exploitation and post-exploitation tooling is disabled for every account, on every plan, until it is switched on for that specific person. Paying does not switch it on. An account nobody has looked at is an account nobody has approved.

The agent enforces this itself, in its main process, using a claim in the signed session token — not with a hidden button in the interface.

Getting it

Ask, with context

Say who you are and what you are testing. Offensive tooling belongs on systems you own or have written permission to attack, and that is the only basis on which it is enabled.

03

Questions worth answering

Trial

What happens on day fifteen?

The agent stops running and tells you why. Nothing is deleted, no card is charged — there is no card. Sign in and the account is still there, waiting for a plan.

Data

What does the server actually see?

Your email and display name, the id and avatar from GitHub or Google if you used them, and a running count of tokens the agent spends. Not your prompts, not your file names, not your code, not your targets.

Keys

Do I pay for model usage twice?

No. REX uses your own provider keys and talks to them directly from your machine. A plan pays for the agent, not for tokens — the token count here exists so usage is visible, not so it can be resold.

Offline

Does it need the internet to run?

Not continuously. It re-validates when it can and keeps working through a grace period when it cannot, so a flight or an air-gapped lab does not stop you mid-engagement.
